# Showrunner Playbook

This file holds the director's standing taste. Claude reads it before every episode, and it is updated as feedback
comes in. It starts with the director's stated principles; add dated entries under **Feedback log** after reviews.

## What makes a Split Studio video work
- **The scenario is the hook.** The first second shows the impossible situation, not a logo or setup text.
  (ep001 opens on one giant athlete facing a sea of 100 toddlers.)
- **Visible action, not narration.** If the narrator says it, the picture must show it. Narration explains rules
  and stakes; it never replaces events.
- **Setup → payoff.** Every joke gets a shot that sets it up and a shot that pays it off, often with a reaction cut.
- **Escalation.** Each beat raises stakes or absurdity. Cut any stretch where nothing new happens (the validator warns on shots over 6 s).
- **Branches reward the viewer.** Red and Blue end in real, different outcomes. No second cliffhanger.
- **Restraint.** Captions for dialogue and narration only. Sound effects where something physically happens. Music
  drops out for important beats (the unison head-turn in ep001, the reveal in ep002).

## Pacing by genre
| genre | shot length | camera | sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| comedy / sports | 0.8–2.5 s, fast cuts on escalation, hold on reactions | tracking, low hero angles, reaction close-ups, slow-mo on the key moment | playful/chase music, crowd, impacts |
| horror / mystery | 1.5–3.5 s, slower, let silence work | static or slow dolly, handheld drift, dutch angle at the reveal | drone, room tone, single sharp sounds, music almost absent |
| physics / what-if | 1.2–3 s, rule → consequence → bigger consequence | wide for scale, repeated identical framings to compare states (the kid's jump at 1G/2G/4G) | rumbles, impacts, data HUD bound to the simulation |

## Hooks that work
- Scale contrast (one vs a hundred, tiny vs giant).
- A familiar situation with one rule broken (gravity doubling, a call from yourself).
- A character the viewer immediately wants to see lose (smug pro).

## Character guidelines
- Give every named character one clear trait that shows in animation (smug arms-crossed, tiny general pointing).
- Faces carry the comedy: switch expressions on beats (smug → confused → terrified).
- Recurring characters live in `bible/characters/`.

## Dislikes (director)
- Bland, generic writing; predictable jokes; empty spectacle; artificial-sounding dialogue.
- Constant sound effects, giant subtitles, camera shake without reason.
- Static images or slideshows standing in for action.
